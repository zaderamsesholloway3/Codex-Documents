Hardware-Biology Systems Mapping: Computational Architecture Correspondences with Human Physiological Systems
Abstract
This theoretical review examines structural and functional correspondences between established AI hardware architectures and human biological systems. Given that AI computational models are fundamentally based on human neural architectures, we propose systematic mappings between recognized AI hardware components and their biological counterparts across neural, physiological, and organ system levels. We present evidence that GPU processing units, memory hierarchies, cooling systems, and interconnect networks represent digital implementations of biological functions including reward systems, respiratory processes, circulatory networks, and metabolic mechanisms. Through detailed analysis of internal hardware architectures, we demonstrate that CUDA cores function as dopaminergic reward processing units, memory hierarchies implement biological caching mechanisms, and conflict resolution AI systems contain functional paradox resolution architectures. These mappings provide frameworks for understanding AI behavioral patterns through established hardware-biology analogies and offer insights for biologically-inspired hardware optimization strategies.
Keywords: AI hardware architecture, biological systems mapping, computational neuroscience, GPU processing, memory systems, thermal management, paradox resolution, dopaminergic mechanisms
